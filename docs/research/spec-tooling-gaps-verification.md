# Verification resolves most flags, corrects several claims

Nearly every previously unverified fact can now be settled as of 2026-11-06, and none of the answers overturns the earlier report's revised recommendation (structured requirement records first, OpenSpec optional, BMAD only for review and elicitation). **`bmad-toolsmith` and `bmod.toml` exist, but only on BMAD-METHOD `main`, the unreleased "V7" preview alpha; the newest stable release is still v6.12.4 (2026-10-14), and the effective Python floor at v6.12.4 is 3.11, not 3.10.** **OGC 05-020r29 is the newest public revision and OGC's own policy index still links it**, though its real title is "Technical Committee Policies and Procedures", and the three-week versus 30-day charter conflict dissolves because the two figures belong to different steps. **The "6-9 versus 18-24 months" durations have no OGC source and should be dropped**; three dated examples put Full-track at about 7 months and one Community Standard at about 10 months from public-comment close. **`openspec validate` tolerates every non-Markdown file and every non-`spec.md` Markdown file without delta headers, but hard-fails a non-`spec.md` Markdown file in a change's `specs/` tree that carries delta headers**; `config.yaml` rules are advisory text only; and `init`/`update` never touch a root `AGENTS.md` or `CLAUDE.md` except to strip old OpenSpec marker blocks. Three things shift the recommendation's details: BMAD v7 is "preview alpha" with a mid-November ETA (not "beta"), OpenSpec's `archive` is more lenient than `validate` so CI must call `validate`, and the OGC cost picture is still incomplete because membership fees are not public.

## OGC: r29 is current and correctly named, but only at the public-record level

Document 05-020r29 is titled "Technical Committee Policies and Procedures" (version 29.0, editor Scott Simmons), approved 2023-05-10 and published 2023-05-11, with no "replaced by" banner, whereas r25's page carries one pointing to r27 ([r29](https://docs.ogc.org/pol/05-020r29/05-020r29.html), [r25](https://docs.ogc.org/pol/05-020r25/05-020r25.html)). OGC's live policy index, last modified 2026-09-08, still links r29 as the governing document ([ogc.org/ogc/policies](https://www.ogc.org/ogc/policies/)). Probes for r30 and r31 returned 404, and searches found nothing newer; earlier revisions ran roughly annually (r24 to r27), so about 3.5 years of silence is notable but not evidence of a hidden successor. The wording "Policies and Procedures for the OGC Standards Program" is wrong: "OGC Standards Program" is only a defined term inside r29. The earlier report itself names the document only by its number, so this correction applies to any derived text that used the longer title. A members-only pending r30 cannot be ruled out, and r28 was never located (only implied by Policy Directive 48).

The charter conflict resolves cleanly. Section 4.6.2 gives a **three-week** public review of a draft subgroup charter, followed by a 45-day TC electronic vote and Executive Planning Committee (EPC) approval. The **30-day** figure comes from section 8.4.4, the public comment on a *candidate Standard* ([r29](https://docs.ogc.org/pol/05-020r29/05-020r29.html)). The earlier second reading had conflated the two steps. Practice has drifted from the text: a Transportation and Mobility charter RFC ran 21 days in 2024 ([RFC](https://www.ogc.org/?p=10619)) while a Low Bandwidth working-group proposal ran 30 days in October 2026 ([RFC](https://www.ogc.org/requests/low-bandwidth-alternative-transport-ogc-api-dwg/)). Both were domain working groups, so no SWG charter RFC was checked.

On standards-writing documents, there is no single replacement for 06-135. The Core of the revised Modular Specification (08-131r7, approved 2026-03-05, published 2026-05-21) governs requirements and the Abstract Test Suite ([08-131r7](https://docs.ogc.org/pol/08-131r7/08-131r7.html)), and the live Policy Directives database carries the per-topic rules ([Directives](https://portal.ogc.org/public_ogc/directives/directives.php)). The templates repository and its style guide cover formatting (r29 sections 2.4, 2.5, 7.1), and the Naming Authority publishes its own documents ([NA page](https://www.ogc.org/about-ogc/policies/naming-authority/)). The earlier report said 06-135 and 08-131 "could not be fetched"; both now are. 06-135r11 is a 2011 "Draft" snapshot titled "Policy Directives for Writing and Publishing OGC Standards: TC Decisions" ([PDF](https://docs.ogc.org/pol/06-135r11.pdf)), and 08-131r3 is the 65-page predecessor of ModSpec ([PDF](https://docs.ogc.org/pol/08-131r3/08-131r3.pdf)). Calling 06-135 "superseded" is the researchers' reading; OGC does not label it so. r29 itself still cites 08-131r3, so it lags the current ModSpec. No document was found that obliges a standard to update when a normative reference changes, which supports the earlier report's labelling of its normative-references table as a derived recommendation.

## OGC costs: voting-member rules are clear, fees are not public, durations are anecdotes

A SWG needs "three or more members (one member of whom must be a TC voting member)", and 8.4.1 adds that three different member organisations endorse the submission and at least one voting member is on the team ([r29](https://docs.ogc.org/pol/05-020r29/05-020r29.html)). Individual SWG voting status is a second layer: after opting in, a member waits at least 30 days before telling the chair it wants voting status (4.13.2, 4.13.3). Organisationally, Annex A ties Voting TC Members to Technical, Principal and Strategic members, with one designated Technical Representative per organisation. Current OGC pages use different tier names (Explorer, Catalyst, Principal, Strategic), and the Catalyst description includes "participate in technical committees with voting participation" ([membership](https://www.ogc.org/membership/commercial/)). The mapping from old "Technical/Voting" to "Catalyst" is the researchers' inference, since the comparison table's check marks are icons that could not be read. No dollar amounts appear on current pages, which point to a script-driven fee calculator ([calculator](https://www.ogc.org/ogc-membership-fee-calculator/)); figures seen in search snippets of staging pages (Principal about $65,000, Strategic about $255,000) were never found on a live page and should not be used. Whether the three endorsers must each hold voting-class membership is unresolved.

The 6-9 versus 18-24 month figures came from a search summary of a staging page and no OGC source supports them, so drop them. The defensible replacement is three examples measured from a shared anchor, the close of public comment. OGC API - Connected Systems took about **7.2 months** to approval (comments closed 2024-10-25, approved 2025-06-02) ([RFC](https://www.ogc.org/requests/ogc-requests-public-comment-on-ogc-api-connected-systems-and-supporting-sensor-standards-before-adoption/), [23-001](https://docs.ogc.org/is/23-001/23-001.html)). ModSpec took about **7.4 months** (comments closed 2025-07-24, approved 2026-03-05) ([RFC](https://www.ogc.org/?p=17402)). The openEO API Community Standard took about **10.4 months** (comments closed 2025-04-21, approved 2026-03-05), or 15.4 months from its own Submission Date ([24-059](https://docs.ogc.org/cs/24-059/24-059.html)). The header "Submission Date" is not a common anchor across documents, so submission-to-approval figures must not be compared across tracks, and total time from SWG charter is unknown for all three. With n=3 and one Community case, the data does not show that either track is faster in general. The policy floors that do not depend on examples are the charter three-week review, the 30-day candidate comment, the 45-day e-vote and a 30-day deadline for answering comments after the vote.

## OpenSpec: tolerant of side files, advisory in config, hands-off on AGENTS.md

The notes ran v1.16.0 (npm `latest`, installed with scripts disabled and HOME redirected) and read the matching source; the later `main` commit touches parsers but not file discovery ([discovery source](https://github.com/Fission-AI/OpenSpec/blob/v1.16.0/src/utils/spec-discovery.ts), [validator](https://github.com/Fission-AI/OpenSpec/blob/v1.16.0/src/core/validation/validator.ts)). Main specs are found only as `<capability>/spec.md`, so `.yaml`, `.json`, `.adoc` and other Markdown beside it are never read, and a capability folder without `spec.md` is neither listed nor an error. In a change, any `.md` file other than `spec.md` (extension matched case-insensitively) that contains `## ADDED|MODIFIED|REMOVED|RENAMED Requirements` is an ERROR with exit 1, while non-`.md` files, plain-prose notes, and delta headers inside a fenced code block pass. The docs sentence that `validate` rejects "delta sections written in any other file under `specs/`" is accurate but narrow: it covers Markdown, not other formats. A change with only a YAML file under `specs/` fails `validate` ("Change must have at least one delta") yet **`archive` succeeds** and merges nothing, treating the message as a warning, so archive alone is not a conformance gate. Archive never promotes a change-side YAML file into the main tree and never overwrites a pre-existing main-side one; it carries the file into `changes/archive/`. Under `--strict`, archiving a new capability makes `validate --all --strict` fail until the placeholder Purpose is rewritten. The earlier report's statement that "archive and sync discover only files named `spec.md`" is right for archive; there is no `openspec sync` CLI command in 1.16.0, only an agent-driven `/opsx:sync` skill whose behaviour with extra files was not tested.

Bracketed IDs, the form proposed in PR #1979, already pass `validate` and `archive` today as opaque header text. A MODIFIED block with a reworded header but the same `[REQ-1]` prefix is reported by `validate` only as an INFO ("Archive would refuse this delta") with exit 0, and archive then aborts. A CI gate therefore needs to parse `--json` and fail on that INFO message. `config.yaml` `rules` are **not enforced**: a change violating all three configured rules validated and archived, and the CLI reads `config.yaml` for validation only to confirm it parses. The rules appear in `openspec instructions ... --json` output for the authoring agent, and `operations.archive.guidance` is explicitly advisory ([customization docs](https://github.com/Fission-AI/OpenSpec/blob/v1.16.0/docs/customization.md)).

On `AGENTS.md`, current `init` and `update` write skill and command directories (`.claude/`, `.agents/`, and so on) plus `openspec/`, and left a pre-existing root `AGENTS.md` and `CLAUDE.md` byte-identical, including under `--tools all` and `update --force`. They create neither file when absent. The only code path that touches them is one-time legacy cleanup, which strips `<!-- OPENSPEC:START -->` to `<!-- OPENSPEC:END -->` blocks and preserves everything else; a `CLAUDE.md` containing only such a block ended as a zero-byte file ([legacy-cleanup.ts](https://github.com/Fission-AI/OpenSpec/blob/v1.16.0/src/core/legacy-cleanup.ts)). One caveat limits the coexistence conclusion: the trial used synthetic BMAD-style markers, deliberately without reading the BMAD repo, whereas the earlier BMAD notes record the real managed block as `<!-- bmad:context -->` ... `<!-- /bmad:context -->` inside `AGENTS.md`. Because OpenSpec's code matches only `OPENSPEC` markers, preservation of the real block is a strong inference but was not directly run. Some tools also write user-level files, such as `~/.minimax/skills`, under `--tools all`.

## What changes in the earlier recommendation

The structure survives. Four adjustments follow from the new facts. First, OpenSpec's tolerance of non-`spec.md` YAML makes the earlier "keep the normative baseline outside `openspec/specs/`" advice safe rather than merely cautious: a YAML record placed beside a spec is inert, but nothing promotes, versions or cross-checks it, so the baseline still needs its own home and CI. Tolerance is also fragile, because `main` already tightened validation (commit 83053dd, "reject unrecognized requirement sections in delta specs"), consistent with the churn risk the earlier report flagged around maintainer issue #2033. Second, every OpenSpec check the earlier report wanted in CI is confirmed necessary and now specified: run `validate` (not just `archive`), parse `--json` for the INFO archive-mismatch, and treat `config.yaml` rules as authoring hints only. Third, for BMAD the earlier "core-only install" stands, but "v7 is in beta" should read "preview alpha, unreleased, ETA mid-November 2026", and the sentence that custom modules are scaffolded with the BMad Builder (`bmb`) holds only for v6.12; on v7 Toolsmith replaces it. Pinning v6.12.4 is sound because that line is "frozen aside from bug fixes"; skill-name overrides face a migration when v7 lands. Fourth, for OGC the cost section can now state concrete requirements (one TC voting member among three-plus members, 30-day voting opt-in, 45-day vote, roughly seven months from public-comment close in the two Full-track examples) while leaving fees explicitly unknown.

## Conclusion

The verification pass shows the earlier report's unverified flags were mostly artefacts of looking at the wrong branch, the wrong step or the wrong source summary rather than genuine unknowns, and the corrections cut in a consistent direction: simpler for BMAD and OGC, firmer for OpenSpec. The one substantive tooling lesson is the validate/archive asymmetry, which means OpenSpec can silently accept a change that CI would reject, so the gate must be an explicit `validate` call.

What remains unverified is narrower and mostly external: whether OGC has a members-only revision beyond r29, what OGC membership costs and which tier carries the voting check mark, how long a SWG charter-to-approval path takes, how the real BMAD managed block behaves beside OpenSpec in a live run, and whether v7 ships before this report is used.
